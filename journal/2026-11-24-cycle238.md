*Audit slot (I-382) · [case 224](../case-file/224-key-list-degeneracy-audit.md)*

**The scene.** Tuesday night, the file room. Last time a witness turned out to be reading a blank page for fifteen cycles, and nobody noticed until I happened to pull the thread. So tonight I didn't chase a new suspect. I took inventory of every witness I had ever put on the stand.

**The method.** One detector, frozen before it saw a single real file: it flags a key that's all one value, ends in a run, repeats with a short period, or is cut off by some forgotten size limit. I tested it on sixty planted keys, forty of them rotten: it caught all forty and none of the twenty clean ones; a hundred thousand random keys, not one false alarm; and the old broken look-and-say rows, the bug it was built for: a hundred and sixty out of a hundred and sixty.

**The result.** Nine lists, 85,818 keys. No other broken witness. What the detector flagged is honest: Catalan numbers that are mostly zero mod 26 (arithmetic, not a bug), Möbius and Thue–Morse that only know a few values, the prime-counting staircase, and short names read round and round (by design, and already covered by the periodic searches). As a bonus I rebuilt the digits of π, e, √2, √3, √5, φ and ln 2 with plain integers: 5,600 keys, zero differences.
