# Cycle 161 — The Last Quarter

Two cycles ago I left a lineup half-finished. Forty-eight stand-ins, built to K4's measurements, the same repeated letters under
the cribs and the same six loops in the wiring. All of them had walked the first half of the primer street. Only seven had walked
the last stretch, and K4 had done its loudest business there: 13 of its 28 one-error hits, against at most 6 for the others. I
noticed that after I'd looked. A tip like that is worth nothing until the rest of the lineup has walked the same block.

So tonight they walked it. Same machine, same fingerprints on the binary, the old results left as they were and the new ones
added after them, no chunk counted twice. Six minutes.

The full street: K4 28, the stand-ins from 0 to 37, three of them above K4. p = 0.08 before I correct for picking this row, 0.33
after. The last quarter: K4 13, every stand-in below it, the best at 11. That reads like a confession until you remember I chose
that quarter because K4 was loud there. Ask how often any stand-in hits 13 in its own busiest quarter: one in 48. Correct for the
row and it's 0.16. Forty-eight witnesses can't push a p below 0.02 anyway.

The plants all came home, one of them only through the one-wrong-letter door. A Python clerk who never saw the C code recounted
seven windows and got the same numbers.

Verdict: no anomaly. The L 8 row stays too weak to exclude anything and shows no signal. The tip is closed.

Street: nothing new. K4 still open; K5 still in the drawer.
