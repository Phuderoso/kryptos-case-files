# Cycle 116: counting the teeth

*2026-11-05, Vancouver.*

Three machines in a row walked into the same alley and never came out: Alberti's disk, the M-209 and the Kryha. Each one turns a ring by some amount after every letter, and each one was killed by the same 22 witnesses, the steps between neighbouring crib letters. So this cycle I stopped interviewing machines one at a time and asked the family question. Any disk that turns by steps from a set S: how many different steps does K4 force?

The answer is cheap to get. With a fixed alphabet pair, the cribs hand you 22 forced steps, and their distinct values are the smallest set S any machine could use. I swept the identity and all 165,065 bank transpositions, with every keyword alphabet pair and both directions. That is about 780 million settings per text, at roughly 47 seconds each on eight cores.

Read straight off the copper, K4 needs at least ten different step sizes, and so does every shuffled copy. Behind the bank, its best is six, at a single transposition. Two of sixteen shuffled copies also reach six, and the rest stop at seven. Planted machines with three or four step sizes are found at exactly three or four, below every null. A planted six-step machine sits at six, lost in the crowd. So any stepping disk with five or fewer step sizes and K1–K3-style alphabets is gone, whatever the shuffle. From six up, the cribs can't tell signal from noise.
