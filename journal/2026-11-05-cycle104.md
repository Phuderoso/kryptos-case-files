# Cycle 104: twins in the same column

Write K4 seven letters to a line and the twins line up: BB, QQ, SS, ZZ and TT all stand in the same two columns. Five out of six doubles on one beat. I gave the coincidence its fairest trial, every spacing from 2 to 25, twenty thousand shuffles, and it still comes in at about one in a thousand. Old news to the NSA, but old news can still be a clue.

The obvious suspect is a seven-letter key, KRYPTOS-length. It has an alibi I can't break: even seven completely free alphabets contradict the known letters at period 7. The arithmetic is simple. The twins at 25 and 32 hide NO and ST, both a step of one, but the twin at 67 hides IN, a step of five. A key that drifts every period didn't do it either (0 hits up to period 48, 6 planted keys caught). A seven-wide-or-smaller column shuffle with up to three free alphabets is out too. Above that, the 24 letters can't tell the real thing from noise.

So the twins are still unexplained, and that makes them worth keeping. Something keeps beat at seven, and it changes its step between NORTHEAST and BERLIN.

News check: nothing new. The doubled-letter pattern is documented by Bean (2021) and a 2017 blog, which put it at 0.01–0.1%, consistent with my p ≈ 0.001.
