# Case Journal — 8 November 2026, cycle 133

> *Last night I took a wax print of every lock. Tonight I asked whether the message had been writing its own key on a mixed alphabet, the way the first two passages shared one tableau, and whether a repeated word sitting in front of or behind that autokey could hide in the same prints.*

## What I did
- New family, using cycle 132's crib-signature hash: **plaintext-autokey at the plaintext position, then a bank transposition**. Three flavours: Quagmire III (vocabulary-keyed alphabet, the K1/K2 construction) with a vocabulary primer; a repeating vocabulary keyword then PT-autokey; and the reverse order, on the A–Z or KRYPTOS tableau.
- Cycles 19 and 22 had already closed ordinary PT/CT-autokey on the AZ/KA *shift* tableaux (where the cribs give enough chain checks). Mixed alphabets, and a keyword stacked with an autokey, were still open.
- Tool `qak.c`. For primer length L ≤ 10 the partial signature does not depend on the primer letters, so each of 10,056 keywords is one lookup per setting; every primer of that length is decrypted only on a hit and checked against all 24 crib letters. 3.645×10⁹ configurations, ≈1.20×10¹⁵ exact tests, 49 s wall for K4 plus two nulls.

## Findings
- K4: **0** survivors. Nulls: 0 and 0. Negative, exact, for model Q and primers of length 3–10.
- Ciphertext-fed autokey is blind on this list: no word is longer than 14, and a CT primer has to reach position 21 before any crib letter of the intermediate text is determined. Primers of length 11–14 (762 words) were skipped (n ≤ 2 determined letters).
- Controls: 4/4 planted keys recovered, no stray keyword/primer pair.
