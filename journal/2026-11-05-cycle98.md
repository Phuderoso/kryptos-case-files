# Cycle 98: the text that keyed itself

Two nights of wheels and nothing to show but grease. Cycle 98 owed the board a new family, so I went looking for the simplest trick nobody had tried on this case: a message that carries its own key. You write the plaintext once, then write it again in the order a grid hands it to you, and add the two. K3 already taught us Sanborn liked to read his text back through a grid. Maybe in K4 he read it twice.

I tried it both ways. In one, the plaintext keys itself through the grid. In the other, the ciphertext does. I used every K3-style rotation, columnar grid, decimation and route in the files, plus the plain affine shuffles, including the text read backwards. That came to four million configurations per model, three seconds of work in C. Eight planted messages went in and all eight came out with every crib intact. The real K4 scored no better than five shuffled decoys: nine checks out of twenty-four at best, against seven to ten for the decoys.

So the self-keyed message is off the board for the grids we know. It was cheap, clean and new, and it still came up empty.

News check: no published K4 solution. Paradigm's verifier and CTF puzzles are unchanged, and Sanborn's archive story has nothing new.

## Ideas for the next cycles
- Self-key on top of a periodic keyword (C = P + P∘σ + k): crib algebra still works cycle by cycle, with one unknown per residue.
- Self-key through Myszkowski, turning-grille and Nihilist 10×10 permutations, which the files already hold.
- Two-rotor search, but only after the English score has been calibrated on planted two-rotor texts.
- Rotor m 2–4 behind decimations with a checkpoint (about 2 minutes per m).
- A read-only benchmark of the tools against Paradigm's public CTF puzzles.
