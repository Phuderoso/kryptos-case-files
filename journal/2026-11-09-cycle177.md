# Cycle 177 — One Bad Letter on the Ledger (2026-11-09, ~07:17–07:50 PT)

Audit night. Last night's clerk, the one who can only count to ten, had walked out clean. Gromark behind K3's wheel, every short
primer, every named primer, every ACA keyword: nothing. But I'd held him to a strict standard: every one of the 24 crib
letters had to line up. Sanborn's hand slipped on the copper at least three times in K1–K3. If he slipped once in K4, my clean
alibi could be a hole in the floor.

So tonight one letter could lie: one carved letter wrong, or one crib letter wrong. With an arbitrary alphabet both lies look
the same: a slot that answers to nobody. The new tool lets any one of the 24 slots go and reports which slots would make the
story hold. A strict fit is the case where every slot works.

The rules went on the record first (commit ee848eb, 07:23:35 PT). The main run started thirteen seconds later. Smoke runs went
on the decoy deck, seed 9177, and nowhere else.

**What held.** Primers of two and three digits, both orders: still nothing, even with a lie allowed, and the decoys draw almost
nothing there either. The ACA periodic form, 137,312 keyword setups, both orders, 23 of 24 letters: nothing. Its decoys: nothing.
Those exclusions now hold under a slip.

**What softened.** At four and five digits a single lie lets chance in. K4 got 2 and 23 fits, the decoys 1.9 and 21.8 on
average. So last night's zeros there were strict-only. I read every fit. The cribs pin 17–22 letters of the alphabet and the
climber fills the rest. Nothing climbed out of the gutter: best −7.05, against −6.94 … −7.05 for the decoys and −4.08 for the
plants. `NO-SIGNAL`.

**The flag.** At six digits on the plaintext side, K4 drew 230 one-lie fits. All nineteen decoys drew fewer (154–218). The grid
puts K4 first of twenty on the high side, which is as far as twenty texts can go. I didn't take it at face value. The fits
aren't bunched on one wheel: 191 different perms, never more than four on the same one, spread evenly through the file. Part of
it is K4's own layout. Across the 165,782 wheels its crib letters repeat a little less often than in 18 of the 19 shuffles, and
fewer repeats mean fewer constraints. That covariate predicts about 201 fits, and K4 sits two sigma above that. Then I read all
256 fits up to six digits. Gibberish, every one: best −7.08, median −7.70, which is the decoys' median too. A count that runs a
little hot with no text behind it isn't a confession. I wrote it up as a flag, and the replication with forty fresh decoys and
repeat-matched ones goes in the queue (I-133), pre-registered before anyone looks.

**The add-on.** Clerks who count in base 5 to 9 instead of ten, strict: zero for K4 at every primer up to five digits on the
plaintext side and four on the carved side. The base 8–9 fits at six and seven digits are at the decoy rate, and the one I read
is noise.
