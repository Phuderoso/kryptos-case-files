# Cycle 160 — Two Strangers at the Table

Quagmire IV is the old trick with two faces: one alphabet for the plaintext, a different one for the ciphertext, a repeating key
between them. Sanborn's K1 and K2 used one face twice. Tonight I asked whether he'd sat two strangers at the table, any two words
from a ten-thousand-word book, before or after any of the 165,065 shuffles in the bank.

First I checked whose ground this was. The SAT proofs from cycle 11b had already shut the door on the copper itself for most
periods, with any alphabets at all. Cycle 28 had paired 268 themed words behind the bank. That's a hundredth of a percent of the
88 million word pairs. The rest was new.

The machine doesn't guess. In every residue class the cribs have to agree on a key letter, which turns into an equation between the
two alphabets. One hash join per lineup covers every pair. Ten and a half million lineups per text, four and a half minutes for
K4 and four shuffled stand-ins.

K4: zero through period 14 in both index orders, zero at 15 with the key on the carved letters, and zero on the bare copper through
period 23, even when I let one crib or one carved letter lie. The stand-ins also gave zero there, and five planted pairs all came
home, including one with a scratched letter that only turns up when the bad crib is set aside. At periods 15–16 the bank coughs up a
few hundred pairs for K4 and as many for the nulls, and every one of K4's reads like a fax machine falling down the stairs: −6.65 at
best, against −4.2 for English.

A Python clerk who shares no code with the machine brute-forced 24 lineups and got the same numbers on every one.

Street: nothing new. K4 still open; Paradigm's verifier still charging a dollar a guess.
