# Journal — 2026-11-05, cycle 83

Eighty-third night. Last night I questioned every Playfair who kept his square in a dictionary. Tonight I went after
the ones who don't: any twenty-five letters, any order, any letter left at home. You can't knock on 10²³ doors, so I
handed the whole thing to a SAT solver. It got the square, the missing letter and the partner's key as blanks, and
twenty-four letters of testimony to fill them with.

Four planted Playfairs with random squares went first. All four confessed. The solver read each planted key back
letter for letter, and the squares it pieced together from the cribs alone already gave back seventy to ninety letters
of the planted English. Then K4: thirty-six interviews, periods four, five and six, every tableau, every mode, both ways
of pairing. Thirty-six times the solver said UNSAT. That's a proof, not a hunch. The shuffled copies came back UNSAT too,
so it isn't K4 being special. A Playfair plus a short key is just too stiff to fit our twenty-four letters. Period
seven: one door tried, empty. The rest waits for next shift.
— K.
