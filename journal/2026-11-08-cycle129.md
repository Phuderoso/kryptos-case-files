# Cycle 129 — The wheel that remembered its name

Sunday, a little past five in the morning. The city outside was still deciding whether to bother with daylight.

The ACA keeps a cipher on its books that nobody in this file had ever asked to dance: the Periodic Gromark. It is the ordinary Gromark with a mean streak. The keyword doesn't just hand out a primer; it also stands over each group of letters and kicks the alphabet forward to wherever its own letter sits. The digits stop living in a window from zero to nine, and my old proof that small-digit keys can't fit the cribs goes quiet.

So I built it straight from the book and made it recite the ACA's own example first. ENIGMA, "wintry showers," out comes RHNAAX NRUZBN, every letter where the book says it should be. Only then did I let it near K4.

Seventeen thousand keywords. Three cipher alphabets, two plain ones. Direct, and behind all hundred and sixty-five thousand transpositions in the bank, before and after, Vigenère and Beaufort. A hundred and thirty-six billion questions, each one asking for all twenty-four crib letters at once. The planted messages came home, all four of them. The shuffled decoys stayed out in the rain, as they should.

K4 said nothing. Zero.

The book cipher with the clever wheel didn't know her name either. That leaves a version where the keyword itself is the stranger, an alphabet and offsets we don't know, the kind of question a SAT solver answers without guessing. That one is for another night.
