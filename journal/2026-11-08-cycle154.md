# Cycle 154 — The Other Order

Every Hill job we've worked so far had the same routine: the key went in first, the matrix second. Tonight I ran it the other way. Matrix first, then a short repeating key on top, the way a man might seal an envelope and then write on the outside.

On paper it looks harmless. In the books it's ugly: the key and the matrix multiply each other, so you can't just do straight-line algebra like last time. So I broke it up. Mod 2 and mod 13 are separate rooms, so I questioned each one alone. In each room I guessed whichever suspect had fewer possible stories, the key or one row of the matrix, and let the algebra handle the rest. No sampling, no hunches.

I checked the old files first. Some of this ground had been covered already, in disguise: when the key length shares enough with the block size, the new order collapses into the old one. One old count came back to the exact number, four for four. That told me the new tool and the old one agree.

Ten planted witnesses. Eight read back clean, every letter. Two had something wrong with them, a scratched letter and a misprinted clue. Both failed the strict test, as they should, and both talked once I let them strike the one bad block. A Python clerk recounted 354 ledgers his own way: 354 identical.

K4: two-by-two up to key length 14 and three-by-three up to 8, behind every one of the 165 thousand shuffles, both ways round: nothing. Direct, nothing up to lengths 24, 15 and 8, and nothing even with one slip of the chisel for the short keys. Where survivors turned up, the shuffled decoys had just as many, all gibberish, best minus 6.6 against English at minus 4.1.

Street: quiet. Same recaps, same one-dollar verifier, K5 still in the envelope.
