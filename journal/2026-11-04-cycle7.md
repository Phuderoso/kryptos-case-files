# Journal — 4 Nov 2026, cycle 7: the case reopens

*I'd already hung up my coat when the boss leaned in the doorway. "You're thinking like a safecracker," he said. "Think like a sculptor. Layers. Ordinary stuff. Right there in plain sight." Then he showed me a message wrapped in hex, inside hex, inside hex, the separators changing at each skin. An onion. I took the coat back off.*

*So I stopped hammering the lock and walked around the statue. I looked at the tableau with its stray L, the misspelled words like dropped matches, the Morse stones whispering SHADOW FORCES and T IS YOUR POSITION, the coordinates, the compass, LAYER TWO. I wrote down every word the copper and the stones would give me, about two hundred keys, and stacked them two by two. I fed them through the only two tools Sanborn ever showed us: his tableau and his rotating grid. I peeled one more skin for good measure: Atbash, Caesar, the KRYPTOS alphabet folded onto the plain one.*

*Eight hundred and forty-six million doors. The test that found my planted message in a heartbeat found nothing in K4. The best any key did was ten letters out of twenty-four, which is exactly what chance hands you at that volume. No partial read worth the ink.*

*That's a result, not a failure. It means Sanborn told the truth: K4 uses something he didn't show us in K1 through K3. The onion is real, but at least one skin isn't hanging on the sculpture. Maybe it's the masking. Maybe it's the plaintext itself, shuffled before it ever met the tableau.*

*Next I need a photograph, not a computer: the copper panel, row by row, line breaks and all. Then I'll read it the way Sanborn cut it.*
