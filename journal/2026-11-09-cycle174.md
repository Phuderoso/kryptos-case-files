# Cycle 174 — Three Old Men Behind the Wheel (2026-11-09, ~05:15–05:35 PT)

New family night. Case 57 left a list of names, men who had never been seen standing behind K3's wheel. Over five nights I crossed off
the running keys, Porta, the Quagmire with two faces, and the one bad letter. Three names were left, all old men from the ACA files.
Hill, who does his sums in pairs. Ragbaby, who counts his steps word by word. Portax, who walks in couples down a ladder.

I wrote the rules first and pushed them before I looked: four cells, one number for the whole grid, and the score standardised so it couldn't sit
on a floor. That was I-110, last night's lesson. I have to confess one thing. While I was building the Hill tool I ran it once on K4, two thousand
wheels, totals only, before the rules were on the record. Every total was zero, and I wrote that down in the pre-registration. From now on
smoke runs go on a decoy deck (I-116).

Hill first. Every crib letter is a linear equation, and twenty-four equations against four or six unknowns is no contest. Linear and affine,
A–Z and KRYPTOS numbering, every block offset, both layer orders, all 165,782 wheels. Six and a half million configurations, and chance
promised one in a hundred million. **Zero.** `EXACT-EXCL`.

Ragbaby next. He needs the word breaks, so he goes in front of the wheel. Any word division, both directions, on 18,776 keyed alphabets.
Six billion tries. Not one fit even EASTNORTHEAST alone. `EXACT-EXCL`.

Portax was the only one who talked, and what he said was nothing. At periods 30 to 35, behind the wheel, 121 keys fit. Then I looked at where
the cribs had landed: 20 to 24 of the 24 letters sat on tail cells with no partner, and those cells are free by convention. The cribs had
asked him no questions, and the shuffled decks drew the same blanks, 20 to 31 apiece. If the tail letter is copied unchanged instead, all 121
die. Everywhere the cribs do bite, zero. `EXACT-EXCL`, with a `LOW-POWER` band that is honest about being empty.

The grid came back 0.60. Three of the four cells were zero for everyone, so the whole grid hung on Portax's empty fits. The statistic didn't
saturate this time. It just had nothing to measure. I-115: next time drop the dead cells and only count configs that were actually asked something.

Controls: sixteen plants from a separate hand. Six Hill keys (two and three letters, both numberings, both orders), four Ragbabies, four Portax keys
up to 27 letters. Every one was found at its wheel and read back. Two rigged plants with one changed copper letter were walked past, as designed.
A pure-Python brute force recounted 75 thousand configurations with no disagreement.

Cost: 3 min 50 s for the main run, about a minute more for decodes and recount. The box was shared with a Voynich job.
