# Cycle 100: many faces, one name

A hundred nights on this case and the clue I'd walked past every time was the flatness. K4's letters are spread too evenly for English. A homophonic cipher does exactly that: it gives the common letters several disguises. With only 26 symbols on the copper, the disguise has to be many-to-one: two or three cipher letters wearing the same plaintext face.

The cribs make that cheap to check. One cipher letter can never be asked to say two different things. Straight on the carved text, it breaks at once. Behind the 340 thousand rearrangements in the bank, nothing passes. Behind every column order up to width thirteen, thirteen and a half billion of them, a few hundred slip through, about as many as on shuffled decoys, and when I let the free letters settle into their best English they still read like a typewriter falling downstairs. My three planted confessions read like confessions at −4.0. K4's best was −4.86, at the decoys' level.

So the many-faced suspect has an alibi too, at least when working alone or behind a plain column grid.
