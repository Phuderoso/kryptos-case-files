Porta was the one name nobody in this office had said out loud. An old Neapolitan trick: split the alphabet in two and every letter has to jump the fence. It doesn't care about the key; it just jumps. So I didn't ask for the key. I asked every shuffling and every themed alphabet a single question: do all twenty-four known letters cross the fence? Fourteen said yes, fewer than most of the shuffled decoys. None of them took a repeating key. Six books read as running keys got halfway and stopped, the same halfway the decoys reach. The planted ones I hid came back every time. — K.
