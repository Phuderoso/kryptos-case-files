# Cycle 178 — The Ledger That Adds Itself (2026-11-09, ~07:50–08:25 PT)

New-family night. The ten-finger clerk is closed. Tonight I went after his cousin, the one who keeps books in base 26. Each new key
letter is a weighted sum of the last few, plus a constant, all mod 26. Fibonacci with a keyword seed is one of his tricks. So are
LFSR-style taps, progressive keys and any short periodic key. Cycle 80 had questioned him on the plain copper and behind the
columnar bank. Nobody had put him behind K3's wheel.

The old interrogation was brute force: try every set of weights and see who blinks. Tonight I let the cribs do the work. With
the key running along the plaintext, the 24 crib letters give 24 key values for each wheel. The recurrence inside each crib block
is then a set of *linear* equations in the weights. Mod 26 isn't a field, so I split the case: one ledger mod 2, one mod 13,
Gaussian elimination in each, and only the handful of surviving books gets carried across the 29-letter gap to BERLINCLOCK. That
reaches nine weights on all 165,782 wheels in seconds. With the key running along the carved letters, the known values are
scattered and there's no local equation. So mod 2 became a sieve: at most 2,048 tiny streams, each one a 97-bit mask, checked in two
machine words. The few wheels that slip through go to the mod-13 clerk, who solves for the seed directly. And for the hand-cipher
story I added the cheap version too: taps of 0 or 1, order 6 to 10, seeded with KRYPTOS, PALIMPSEST, ABSCISSA, BERLINCLOCK and
seventy other words from the case.

The rules went on the record first (202c378 and dd29766, about 07:59 PT). Smoke went on the decoy deck, seed 9178, and nowhere else.

**What happened.** Nothing. Zero fits for K4 in every cell: plaintext side up to nine weights, carved side up to five, the
vocabulary-seeded taps up to ten, both orders, both tableaux, Vigenère and Beaufort. Nineteen shuffles: zero as well. At these
sizes chance can't produce a fit (about one in twenty at nine weights over the whole file), so the zeros are exact exclusions,
not low power. The mod-2 sieve counts, which I kept as a diagnostic, sit right on the decoys' average.

**Controls.** Twelve plants behind the wheel, every one found at its own wheel with its own weights, and every one read back to
the planted sentence: a Fibonacci key seeded KR, a key with a non-invertible last weight, PALIMPSEST and BERLINCLOCK seeds. The
KRYPTOS plant also turned up as ABCDEF…, which is the same seed spelled in the other numbering. One negative failed, and I kept
the failure. I'd planted a three-weight key with one wrong carved letter at slot 25 and said it must be missed at every order.
It was missed up to seven. At eight and nine it fit. That's no bug in the solver. At order eight the first equation starts at
slot 29, so slot 25 only enters through weights four to eight, and those are zero in the true key. The lie sits in a room nobody
walks through. A post-hoc negative with the lie at slot 30, inside every seed window, was missed at every order. Lesson for the
next audit: say which slots carry weight at each order. A numpy brute force that shares no algebra with the C code matched the
slice line for line, solution counts included.
