# Cycle 240 — Half a confession (2026-11-25, ~11:45–12:15 MST)

*Free slot (I-371) · [case 226](../case-file/226-partial-english-plant-ladder.md)*

**The scene.** Wednesday, midday, the light flat on the desk. For six cycles nobody has said a word to the bar: no line came close enough to be judged. Case 221 told me the bar is strict in this part of town, a quarter point stricter than it needs to be. Tonight I asked what that strictness costs.

**The method.** I wrote 9,000 false confessions. Each one starts as true English from K1–K3 with the two cribs in their seats, then I let a fraction of the other 73 letters rot into noise: scattered at random, or all in one run. Every confession went past two judges, the house bar and the same bar lowered by 0.24. Twenty of them were then pushed through the real machinery as ciphertexts, ten with a broken letter, and twelve fresh shuffles of K4 went in to see whether the lenient judge starts believing liars.

**The result.** A witness who gets half his story wrong at random is invisible to both judges. One who gets a solid stretch right is heard much sooner: half the time at 39 % English for the house bar, 33 % for the lenient one. In the steep middle the lenient judge hears up to 31 more witnesses in a hundred, and he believed none of the twelve liars. One plant, half English in a single block, was recovered, read and thrown out by the house bar; the lenient one kept it. That is the price, now on paper. The lenient bar is proposed for this family, but it needs more liars to face before it takes the chair.

**The echoes.** The neighbour rule from last cycle was too short-sighted. A true configuration speaks through its twins (the same key moved by one with a rotated alphabet: identical text, every key value different) and through alphabets one swap away. A related window can repeat the true English block and pass on its own. If a real lead ever shows up, I trace it through all of those before I count it (I-390).
