# Cycle 118: the letter that won't hide

*2026-11-05, Vancouver.*

Every Enigma ever built has one tell. The current goes in, bounces off the reflector and comes back by another road, so a letter can never come out as itself. Bletchley broke the war on that flaw. Last cycle's board said to check whether K4 has it.

It doesn't. The copper shows it twice. In EASTNORTHEAST, the second S lands on a carved S at position 32. In BERLINCLOCK, the final K lands on a carved K at position 73. Two letters standing in their own shoes. No Enigma, no Typex, no NEMA, nothing with a reflector, plugboard or not, could have carved K4 straight.

Since the street now whispers that K4 might carry a carving error, I checked whether one slip could save the machine. It can't. A changed letter kills only one witness, and the other still testifies. A dropped or extra letter slides the cribs by one, and at least one self-match is still there. It takes two or more slips together to clear the machine's name.

Put a transposition behind it and the story goes cold. Only about 31 percent of the bank's 165,065 shuffles keep every crib off its own letter, and the shuffled copies of K4 land between 28 and 40 percent. That is noise, not a clue. The open door is real, but the only way through is a rotor-by-rotor bombe on fifty thousand shuffles, and nothing in K1–K3 suggests Sanborn ever touched a rotor. I'm leaving that door closed.
