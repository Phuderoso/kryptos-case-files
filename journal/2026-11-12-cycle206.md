# Cycle 206 — The Clerk Who Shuffled His Pages (2026-11-12, ~15:44–16:02 PT)

Last week's suspect was a clerk who starts his key over at every word. We shook down his desk in cycle 203: on a plain tableau,
straight onto the copper, he couldn't have done it with any key shorter than nineteen letters. Tonight's question was whether he
covered his tracks the K3 way, shuffling the page after he wrote it, or kept a second alphabet in the drawer.

**The shuffle.** Every one of the 165,065 transpositions in the bank, both ways round, two tableaux, three forms, every way the cribs
can split into words, every place the first word could have started. Thirty-odd billion little alibis. Before I looked at K4 I made
the tool find two planted clerks hiding behind entries 12,345 and 98,765; it found both, keys and all. Then K4: nobody, at any key length
up to seventeen. Twenty shuffled copies of K4 run through the same bank were just as empty there, so the zero means something. From
eighteen up the shuffles start finding clerks too, and K4's handful looks like theirs: strings like AWDMLCFFYK in front of EASTNORTH.
That's noise wearing a hat.

**The second alphabet.** With the plain side or the cipher side free, or both, the solver can't make the cribs fit below eleven,
ten and five letters of key. Random pages give out at about the same lengths. So those doors are shut, but they'd be shut on almost
any text; above them the room is too big to search by cribs alone. I nearly fooled myself first: the fixed alphabet was pinned with
half a clamp and Quagmire I came up "possible" at one letter of key, which is a plain substitution K4 rules out by eye (N and O both go
to Q). Killed the run, fixed the clamp, added that check as a gate, ran again.

**The desk across the hall.** Voynich finished the bookkeeping on our W rectangle: count every symmetric shape anyone could have
gushed about, and K4's W is ordinary, p 0.58. A stricter mirror keeps it there (0.52). Only the exact inset-with-centre shape is rare,
and that's the shape you'd pick after seeing it. Logged in case 192 with credit; I checked their registration hashes, not their shuffles.
The "W as a stop" card slides down a notch.
