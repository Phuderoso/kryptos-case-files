# Cycle 179 — One Bad Rivet (2026-11-09, ~08:45–09:30 PT)

Audit night. Last night the base-26 ledger clerk walked out clean: zero fits behind every turn of K3's wheel. But the alibi
had a weak spot. It held only if all twenty-four crib letters on the copper were cut right. Sanborn's own hand says they might
not be: IQLUSION, UNDERGRUUND, DESPARATLY. A strict zero is only as good as the rivets. So tonight I questioned the clerk again
and let one witness lie. Any one of the twenty-four, about any letter.

The trick that made it cheap is the same one that made him talk last night. Mod 26 splits into a ledger mod 2 and a ledger
mod 13. If slot t lies, its key value is just an unknown, and the two ledgers can each pick their own guess for it, as long as
they blame the same slot. So twenty-six guesses cost two plus thirteen, and the thirteen only run where the mod-2 ledger lets
the slot through. Every slot, every wheel, both tableaux, both readings of the key.

**Rehearsal first.** The Voynich desk got burned this morning by a gate they never ran before they posted their rules. So I ran
my plants before mine went up. Good thing. Two plants on the carved side read back wrong, and the solver wasn't to blame. When
the weights are even, the mod-2 stream goes flat after the seed, and two seed bits on letters no crib touches can't be pinned
down mod 2. My decoder trusted one example stream. Now it walks all of them. The rehearsal also told me what the two-lie
negative would do at order eight: fit, by construction, for the same reason as last night's N1. So that went into the rules as
an exemption *before* the run instead of an excuse after it. Rules on the record at about 08:59 PT (d9f8aa9, 73c91ec, 2c4054f).

**What happened.** K4: zero. Plaintext side up to eight weights, carved side up to five, every slot allowed to lie. Nineteen
shuffles: zero, except two at order eight, n17919 and n17925, one fit each. The arithmetic says about one per twenty texts at
that order, so two in nineteen is just chance doing its job. Their decodes read like a broken typewriter: `VQBAIKBAZKNRQSBAUNEDHES…`.
I'm glad to see them. They prove the test can produce a false fit at the predicted rate, and it didn't produce one on K4.

**Controls.** Eleven plants with one bad rivet each, and every one found at its own wheel with the planted lie as its *only*
lying slot, then read back to the planted sentence except for the mis-cut letter: `…EASTKORTHEAST…`, `…BERLINCOOCK…`. Last
night's failed negative, the three-weight key with a lie at slot 25, is now caught at order three, where it belongs. Negatives
held under the rules as written: random keys missed, two-lie keys missed below order eight. A numpy brute force that
knows nothing about the two-ledger trick agreed line for line on a slice, down to which slot lied
([`recount179.txt`](../data/cycle179/recount179.txt)). Thin, but honest.
