# Cycle 144 — A Coat That Doesn't Fit

Last cycle I dressed the matrix in a coat cut to its own size: one shift per row. Too neat. Sanborn never tailored
anything that tidily. So this time the coat came in any length — a running key of period one to fourteen, worn over
blocks of two, three, four.

No guessing tonight. Every unknown went into one ledger — the matrix entries, the key letters — and the twenty-four
letters we know did the bookkeeping. Ninety-eight million settings per text. For blocks of two, the books never
balanced, not once. Blocks of three and four let a few hundred through, and every one read like the shuffled decoys:
−7.2 at best, where English sits near −4.1.

The six planted messages all came back clean, so the net was whole. Three minutes of compute, and one more family
goes into the closed drawer.
