# Cycle 190 — One Bad Letter (2026-11-09, ~15:20–15:45 PT)

Last night's suspect walked because the alibi was airtight: twenty-four crib letters, every one checked. But Sanborn is a man who carved
IQLUSION and UNDERGRUUND, and left an S off K2. If one letter in the crib window is wrong, every strict test I ran last night is blind. Tonight
I pulled the file back out and asked the question with one letter allowed to lie.

**The tool.** `cmp190.c` takes case 176's union-find and lets it throw out any one crib equation. The hard part was the meter. One error buys
a lot of chance fits, and I wanted the exact number, not a guess. Edges that sit on exactly the same loops are interchangeable witnesses:
throw out any one of them and you get the same story. So the chance is one term per *class* of witnesses, not per letter. I checked that
arithmetic on forty random texts before K4 came near the room. It matched in every band. The Voynich desk had offered a brute-force
fixture for exactly this kind of meter; the forty texts are that fixture.

**The plants (11/11).** Six two-pass texts with one bad letter and one clean one, all found at their own route, pair and slot. The rehearsal
caught two of my own mistakes before the push: the solver names the first witness of a class, not necessarily the planted one, and a
two-error negative with both errors in one class is really a one-error text. Both fixed and written down before any K4 run.

**The run (15:26–15:29 PT).** A–Z and KRYPTOS behind all 165,782 routes: every pair that still has power with one error (63 on the plain
index, 46 on the carved one, 8 × 10 and 7 × 11 among them) came back clean. Direct with 18,824 keyed alphabets: clean. The 144 themed
alphabets on every 512th route coughed up one fit in a borderline cell; both of its readings were noise (`ITETFBAXXRQOBIJJ…`). Nineteen
shuffles sat around K4, the independent recount agreed fit for fit, and the chance meter matched the totals to a few parts in a million.
**`EXACT-EXCL`, one-error scope.**
