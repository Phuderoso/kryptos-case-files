# Cycle 127: ten pigeonholes and a number

*2026-11-08, Vancouver.*

It was a new-family night, so I went looking for a door with no fingerprints on it. I found one in the ACA's back rooms: the Sequence Transposition. You take five digits and let them breed by chain addition, the way a Gromark key does. Every digit sends one plaintext letter into one of ten pigeonholes. Then you empty the pigeonholes in the order a keyword gives. It's a running number hidden inside a transposition, simple parts stacked on simple parts, which is just how Phuderoso thinks this thing was built.

The numbers were rude: a hundred thousand primers times three point six million column orders. Brute force with no thought behind it would have taken all week. So the solver walks the column orders one column at a time and drops a branch the moment two crib letters ask for different key letters at the same position in the cycle. I ran it against every period up to fourteen at once, key before or after, on both alphabets.

The four planted messages came back with their primers and keywords: GUMMYBEARS, CHARTHOUSE, BROADCLOTH, KRYPTOSABC. K4 went through all hundred thousand primers in four minutes and gave back nothing. The shuffled copy gave back nothing too. That makes the zero mean something.

Its September 29 status list still names Quagmire with two different keyed alphabets and Gromark with keyed alphabets as not ruled out in its published record. Our case file 27 (Quagmire IV behind transposition) and cycle 65 (arbitrary-alphabet Gromark, primer length 5–6) cover part of that.
