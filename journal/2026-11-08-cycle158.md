# Cycle 158 — Two Names on the Door

Two nights ago I hung a lock on each side of the Hill matrix and found the door shut, but only for short keys. Long keys had too many
moving parts: with 24 known letters you can't pin down a 4-entry matrix and two 12-letter keys at once.

Sanborn never used random keys, though. K1 opened with PALIMPSEST, K2 with ABSCISSA. So tonight I made both locks words. Fix the two
words and the only stranger left in the room is the matrix, and each of its rows answers to its own crib letters. A dozen equations for two
unknowns doesn't leave much room for luck.

I ran every word in the 10,056-word book against every other, Hill sizes 2, 3 and 4, straight on the copper. Nothing. Behind all 165,065
transpositions I used the 32 words that matter most (KRYPTOS, PALIMPSEST, ABSCISSA, BERLINCLOCK, IQLUSION and the rest). Nothing at sizes
2 and 3. At size 4 the cribs get thin in the carved order and luck gets a vote: 44 hits for K4, about the same rate as the shuffles, every
one of them gibberish.

The plants came home, all ten of them, including the one with a bad carved letter and the one with a wrong crib. A Python clerk who knows
nothing about null spaces brute-forced the matrix rows and got the same counts, number for number.

Street: nothing new. K4 open, Paradigm's verifier still taking dollars, K5 still sealed.
