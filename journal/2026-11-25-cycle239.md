# Cycle 239 — Say what you see (2026-11-25, ~03:38–04:00 MST)

*New family (I-379) · [case 225](../case-file/225-audioactive-digit-streams.md)*

**The scene.** Wednesday, before dawn. Two cycles ago I fixed a witness who had been reading blank pages: the look-and-say lengths. But I only ever asked him how *long* his story was. Tonight I asked him to say it.

**The method.** Look-and-say is a rule a child can run on a napkin: read the last line aloud, "one one, two ones, one two one one…", and write what you said. The digits are only ever 1, 2 and 3, so as a key they are small shifts. Conway showed the strings fall apart into 92 "elements" that never touch again; I derived them in code and checked them against his table, all 92, and every decay. Then 6,810 keys: the strings themselves, their digits read in pairs, the element numbers, the first digit of each element, and the length numbers read two digits at a time. The degeneracy detector was run first and its verdict pasted into the preregistration before any key met K4.

**The result.** Nothing. Not a single setting puts 23 of the 24 known letters where Sanborn said they are, direct or behind 165 thousand transpositions. Twenty planted messages came back, all twenty, ten of them with a deliberately broken letter. Six shuffled copies of K4: nothing. One thing the plants taught me: in a three-valued stream, a key's neighbour can read half a message too. If a real lead ever shows up in a list like this, I read it through every neighbour before I believe it (I-386).
