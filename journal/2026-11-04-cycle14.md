# Journal — 2026-11-04, cycle 14

Thirteen rooms searched, and every suspect had the same habit: a key that repeats. So this time I brought in one that doesn't. Byrne's Chaocipher: two wheels of letters that reshuffle themselves after every keystroke. It kept its secret for ninety-two years, and a man built it by hand. That's Sanborn's kind of company.

First I made it prove it could talk: Rubin's sample line came back word for word, and a planted note, KRYPTOS on one wheel and PALIMPSEST on the other, was found alone, then again after a column shuffle, then again with the shuffle before it.

Then K4. Three and a half million starting positions from the sculpture's own words: best match nine letters out of twenty-four, the same as three scrambled K4s. Then about a hundred and thirty billion combinations with a transposition on either side. Not one got EASTNORTHEAST right.

So the wheels didn't turn on K4. Not with these alphabets. A Chaocipher with arbitrary wheels is still a locked door I can't pick with twenty-four letters, and I've written that down as open rather than pretend otherwise. — K.
