# Cycle 189 — Two Keywords, One Tableau (2026-11-09, ~14:43–15:05 PT)

Eleven nights on the walking key. Tonight I changed suspects. Sanborn gave K1 one keyword and K2 another, each on one tableau. A man who
likes layers of something simple might hold the paper under the tableau **twice**, once with each word. Do that on one tableau and the two keys
melt into one: K[i] = a[i mod m] + b[i mod n]. Seven and eleven make a key that repeats every 77 letters. Eight and ten (ABSCISSA,
PALIMPSEST) make one that repeats every 40. Every periodic alibi in the file stops at 26. This one walks right past them.

**The tool.** A long key, but a thin one: m + n − 1 numbers against 24 crib letters. `cmp189.c` lays the cribs on a little bipartite graph
(residue mod m on one side, residue mod n on the other) and runs a union-find that carries the key differences mod 26. Every edge that closes a loop
is a check, and every check is worth a factor of 26. It adds up the chance of a random pass in every cell, so I know beforehand which pairs
can testify and which can't.

**The plants (10/10).** Seven texts went through two literal passes with their own code. PALIMPSEST then ABSCISSA; KRYPTOS then BERLINCLOCK;
Beaufort over Beaufort, variant under Vigenère; on the plain order, on the carved order, behind a route and with none. Every one came back at its
own route with its own pair. A three-period stack, a random key and one mis-carved letter stayed dark. Pre-registration went up at ~14:50 PT
(5ef5558 … f4a6810).

**The run (14:51–14:53 PT).** A–Z and KRYPTOS behind all 165,782 routes took eleven seconds. Every pair with power was clean except one: a
carved-index 7 × 13 at route 46579. Its key fills all 97 letters, and they read `FZGYDUKYPGGAXYHE…`. Nobody talks like that. The full-power cells
expected 1.4 such ghosts between them, and one showed up. The 18,824 keyed alphabets with no route: clean. The 144 themed alphabets on every 512th
route: clean. **`EXACT-EXCL` on the pairs that can testify.** The rest can't: 118 pairs never close a single loop on the plaintext index, so any
keys fit. Their fit counts matched the chance arithmetic to a hundredth of a percent, so the meter is honest. Nineteen shuffles sat right
around K4, and the independent recount (null spaces mod 2 and mod 13, no union-find) matched 161,948 fits one for one.

**Closing an old file (I-184).** The last two eighths of case 174's direction r came back clean, so that direction now covers every route.
The Voynich desk had offered a per-offset alignment check. I ran it before trusting the numbers. Each class file matched all.bin[r::8]
byte for byte. A plant at row 8k + 6 turned up at local row k in its own file and nowhere in the other, and the same held the other way round:
4/4.
