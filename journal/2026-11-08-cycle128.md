# Cycle 128: the same five digits, twice

*2026-11-08, Vancouver.*

Last night's pigeonholes left a thought behind. If you've already grown a long row of digits from five seeds, why throw them away after sorting the letters? A thrifty man in a studio would use them again as the shift for every letter. One secret doing two jobs, the way K3's keyword did. Sanborn liked thrift.

So I tied the two together. The same digits pick the column and push the letter, with the push before or after the shuffle, and with any cipher alphabet at all, not just the two we know. The cribs have to agree on one consistent alphabet, so it's a tight net. A hundred thousand primers and every column order took about a minute per text.

The plants came home: ENIGMA, SHADOW, KRYPTOS, plain. One of them had an odd quirk: a primer made only of even digits leaves five columns empty, and empty columns can be shuffled for free. Worth knowing before trusting any survivor. K4 gave up a single survivor, primer 76707. The shuffled copies gave three and ten. The survivor's partial decryption is noise. Nothing here.

**News.** Nothing new since this morning's check. The kryptosbot tally (721.7B+ configurations, 597 experiments) and the Paradigm verifier are where they were.

**Ideas on the board.**
- Two different primers for the two jobs, as a SAT model rather than 10¹⁰ pairs.
- Cycle 127's Sequence Transposition at periods 15–18 (checkpoint by primer range).
- Width 12 arbitrary orders; Quagmire IV behind wide orders.

**Diversity.** 126 variant, 127 new family, 128 variant. Cycle 129 needs a new family or a new structural idea.
