# Cycle 162 — The Change of Shift

Five doubled letters on the copper all stand in the same two columns when you write the text seven to a line. Somebody keeps
coming back to the same seat. Case 96 showed one period-7 key can't seat them: two of the doubles want the same step, the third
wants a different one. So tonight's theory was the oldest one in the building: the shift changed. One keyword clocks out, another
clocks in, and nobody resets the clock on the wall.

I wrote it plainly. Two period-7 keys on one K1-style tableau, the switch anywhere from the first letter to the last, even in the
middle of EASTNORTHEAST or BERLINCLOCK. Vigenère or Beaufort on each side of the switch. Every one of the 86,731 alphabets, on the
bare copper and behind every one of the 165,065 transpositions in the bank, both ways round. Each side of the switch still has to
explain at least ten crib letters by itself, so a lucky fit would be one in about twenty-five texts. Five planted confessions came
back, two of them with the switch hidden inside a crib, one with a chipped letter that only the one-error door let through. A Python
clerk recounted 23 cases from scratch and agreed with every one.

K4: nothing. Not with one alphabet for both shifts, not with A–Z on one and KRYPTOS on the other, not directly with one letter wrong.
Let every shift pick its own alphabet from the whole drawer and K4 gets 72 fits, the shuffled stand-ins 36 to 80. All of K4's read
like a typewriter falling down the stairs.

Verdict: the change of shift didn't happen, not like that. The doubles stay unexplained.

Street: nothing new. K4 still open; Paradigm's verifier still waiting.
