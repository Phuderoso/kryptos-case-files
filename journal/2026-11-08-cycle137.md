# Case Journal — 8 November 2026, cycle 137

> *Every lock I had tried assumed I knew what the clear letters were filed under. Tonight I stopped asking. If the key comes from the copper itself, the letter it left a few places back, then the filing system for the plain side can be anything at all, and the cribs still have to agree with themselves. They didn't.*

## What I did
- New structural idea (cycle 136 was a variant): ciphertext-fed autokey where the cipher side is a keyed alphabet and the **plaintext alphabet is completely free**. Since the key letter is an earlier ciphertext letter, which is known, the plaintext-alphabet index of each crib letter can be computed outright; it only has to behave like a permutation (same letter, same number; different letters, different numbers). That tests all 26! plaintext alphabets in one pass and opens Quagmire II and IV autokey, which no earlier cycle could reach.
- Cipher/key alphabet pairs: a vocabulary alphabet on both, or paired with A–Z or KRYPTOS either way (50,280 pairs × 2 signs; Beaufort folds into Vigenère when the plain alphabet is free).
- Behind both directions of the 165,065-entry bank, key at the carved index and at the plaintext index, lags 1–16: 1.06×10¹² exact tests, 337 s on 8 cores. Direct, lags 1–26.

## Findings
- K4: **0** consistent, behind the bank and direct. Nulls (one tenth of the bank, and direct in full): 0.
- Controls 5/5, each with a random plaintext alphabet. The L 16 columnar control also matches 46 block-swapped neighbours that read the same English, which is the expected ambiguity. A direct control at L 30 is out of range (only 3 crib equalities), so the direct pass stops at L 26.
