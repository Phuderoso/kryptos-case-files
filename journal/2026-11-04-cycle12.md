# Journal — 2026-11-04, cycle 12

The client asked for a profile, not a suspect. So I sat with K1, K2 and K3 the way you sit with a man's old letters. Same habits every time: one real word for a key, a word that talks about the trick itself. One alphabet doing two jobs. One letter out of place, never more. And a change of method between rooms, like a man who never takes the same street home twice.

That gave me a picture of K4: a scrambled page, then a familiar alphabet, maybe digits instead of letters. So I tried to unscramble first. Columns in every order up to eight wide, routes, K3's own double turn of the grid, even the version where the stonecutter dropped a letter, the way he dropped K2's S. Ninety-six thousand ways to put the furniture back, twenty-one thousand alphabets.

The planted body turned up exactly where I'd buried it. K4 gave me nobody under base nine, and above that only the usual crowd that a shuffled K4 also draws.

Off the wire: a write-up from October says the Smithsonian pages were strips of text taped back out of order. Strips, not columns. That's where I go next, along with keys that name their own machinery: CLOCK, BASE, ZEIT. — K.
