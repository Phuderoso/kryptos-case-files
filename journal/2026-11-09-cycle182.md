# Cycle 182 — Two Hands on One Wheel (2026-11-09, ~11:17–11:45 PT)

Two nights running the clerk wrote in a borrowed hand: one keyword alphabet, used on both sides of the counter. Tonight I asked
the question a real forger would have made me ask. What if he signs with one hand and counts the change with the other? That's
Quagmire IV. One keyed alphabet takes the plaintext, a different one gives the ciphertext, and the same marching recurrence key
runs underneath, behind every one of K3's 165,782 wheels.

**The trick.** The two hands look like they multiply the work: 144 themed alphabets times 18,776 vocabulary alphabets is 2.7 million
pairs per wheel, both ways round. But mod 2 the hands never meet. The key bit is the parity of the cipher hand at the carved letter
XOR the parity of the plain hand at the crib letter. One half changes with the wheel. The other half never changes. So I folded the
half that never changes into the ledger before the night started. When the plain hand is the short list, it's one big map of every
word that could pass for some plain hand. When it's the long list, it's 128 little indexes, one per mod-2 rule, keyed on a linear
fingerprint. Either way it's about nineteen thousand questions per wheel, not 2.7 million. And the orders nest: a story that holds
at order three also holds at order six. So the mod-13 clerk only gets asked once per near-miss. 1.8 trillion configurations took 78
seconds.

**Rehearsal first.** Fourteen plants went in before the rules went up. Ten were real Quagmire IV wheels in both directions, at orders
one to six, including a non-unit last weight, a progressive key, and a cipher hand that only exists in the themed list
(UNDERGRUUND). Four were decoys: one mis-carved letter, a random key, a pair outside the lists, and an order-seven key. 14/14, and an
audit build that asks every single pair the slow way agreed line for line. Rules on the record ~11:28 PT (b7527cc … 553efc3).

**What happened.** K4: zero, at every order up to six, both directions, on every wheel. Nineteen shuffles: zero. At order six chance
would hand out about one fit per eighty texts, so the zero has teeth. A numpy brute force that never heard of tables, nesting or
fingerprints agreed on all six texts it saw, including zero for K4. The near-miss counts put K4 in the middle of the shuffles
(grid p⁺ 0.55, p⁻ 0.35). Nothing to decode.

**The old smudge, closed.** Last cycle's carved-side near-miss count sat on the floor for the second time. Tonight it got the test I
promised: forty fresh shuffles, plus twenty that keep every letter's A–Z parity in place. Two of forty beat K4 (p 0.073), and two of
twenty matched ones did too (p 0.143). Not replicated. The matched null came with a free alibi check. The q1 part depends only on
that parity pattern, so it had to come out identical in all twenty, and it did. The drawer is shut.
