# Case Journal — 8 November 2026, cycle 134

> *Last night the wax prints only named primers short enough to hide in front of the first crib. Tonight I glued dictionary words together until the primer itself stepped on EASTNORTHEAST, and I asked the locks whether a long phrase had been writing the key all along.*

## What I did
- Variant of cycle 133: the same model-Q families (Quagmire III autokey, and a themed repeating keyword stacked with an autokey in either order), now with **phrase primers** — two vocabulary words joined, three themed words, and the 762 leftover words of length 11–14 that cycle 133 skipped.
- PT-autokey at L 11–22 (where the hash filter of cycle 133 is underpowered) and CT-autokey at L 22–28 (where the primer finally overlaps the first crib). The solver derives the primer from the 24 crib chains on each of 330,130 permutations and looks it up in the phrase set, instead of hashing a primer-independent signature.
- Unique primers: 88,797,091 (PT) and 1,950,051 (CT). About 3.055×10¹² configurations, ≈1.008×10¹⁸ exact tests. Wall time 678 s (8 cores) for K4 plus two nulls.

## Findings
- K4: **0** survivors (and 0 crib-consistent derived primers). Nulls: 0 and 0 phrase hits (null 1 produced 4 consistent primers that were not phrases). Negative, exact, for model Q in the lengths above.
- Controls: 4/4 planted keys recovered. The CT control (L = 23) correctly returns a cluster of crib-consistent phrases that share the two determined primer letters; the planted phrase is the one that decrypts the whole English plaintext.
