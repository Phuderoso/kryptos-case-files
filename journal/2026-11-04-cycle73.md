# Journal — 2026-11-04, cycle 73

Seventy-third night. I walked past the copper stones at the door, the ones that have been tapping SHADOW FORCES in dots
and dashes since 1990, and asked whether K4 was simply Morse folded into threes. It is a pretty idea: the separators are
X's, the trick is in plain sight, the stones are on the same grounds.

It dies on arithmetic. Eleven carved letters hold thirty-three dots, dashes and gaps; BERLIN CLOCK in Morse needs
forty-four. EAST NORTH EAST squeezes into its thirteen letters with no room to spare — a coincidence that almost felt
like a wink. And without word gaps, the cipher can only ever print twenty-two different letters; K4 prints all
twenty-six. The SAT solver, asked the softer question, shrugged: anything passes it, shuffled K4 included. I wrote that
down as "no evidence", not as a lead. — K.

**Result:** [case file 71](../case-file/71-fractionated-morse.md). Fractionated Morse excluded by a counting proof under
the positional cribs (BERLINCLOCK needs 44 symbols, 33 available) and, for unspaced plaintext, by alphabet size (≤ 22 letters
vs K4's 26). Spaced crib-free SAT: K4, controls and nulls all SAT → inconclusive.
