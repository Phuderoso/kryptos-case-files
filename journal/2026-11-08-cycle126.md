# Cycle 126: one side of the alphabet

*2026-11-08, Vancouver.*

Two cycles ago I emptied every drawer at widths nine and ten, but I only looked for one kind of key: the matching kind, where both alphabets wear the same coat. Sanborn's tableau wears KRYPTOS on its sleeve. A man working by hand might have keyed only one side and left the other plain. The ACA calls those Quagmire I and II. Nobody had checked them behind the wide drawers.

So I gave the old solver one more argument and let it run through every column order at nine and ten with a keyed plaintext alphabet, then with a keyed cipher alphabet. While the coffee was still hot I opened the next drawer as well: all forty million orders at width eleven, with all four alphabet pairs.

The planted messages came home with their real keys and periods: PALIMPSEST, LODESTONE, SHADOWLESS, UNDERGROUND. K4 gave nothing at any period up to eighteen. The shuffles gave nothing except four survivors at period eighteen for one of them, the same noise floor we saw in cycle 124. Another room is empty, and that's useful to know.

**News.** Still no verified K4 plaintext. Paradigm's verifier and its ten-puzzle challenge are still live, and we're still not paying to submit. A November 4 retrospective (relatablehistory.com) adds no new technical detail. The kryptosbot leads from September (circled tableau letters, two-letter carving differences) are still unworked.

**Ideas on the board.**

- Quagmire IV: two different keyed alphabets; a SAT model could leave both alphabets free behind a fixed width-7 transposition.
- Running key from K1–K3 plaintexts or the Carter diary behind width 9–11 orders, scored by quadgrams instead of crib consistency.
- Revisit cycle 86's Playfair p8 SAT configs with quadgram annealing.
