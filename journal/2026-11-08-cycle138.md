# Case Journal — 8 November 2026, cycle 138

> *Last night the clerk filed the key under the copper's own index. Tonight I made him look the key up in the same drawer he used for the clear letters, a drawer whose order nobody knows. Every crib becomes a little equation between two unknown drawers. Straight off the wall, none of the systems close. Behind the shuffles they close all the time, the way loose chains always close, and nothing they unlock reads like English.*

## What I did
- Variant of cycle 137: ciphertext-fed autokey where **the key letter is read through the free plaintext alphabet** `x`, not through a known tableau. SUM `y(c) = x(p) + x(k)` and DIFF `y(c) = x(k) − x(p)` (Beaufort and variant coincide when `x` is free).
- An exact solver for the resulting two-variable equations mod 26: components with one free root each, roots fixed by cycle equations, and backtracking so that `x` stays a permutation.
- Direct, all 10,056 vocabulary cipher alphabets, L 1–26 (2.09×10⁶ exact tests). Behind both directions of the 165k bank, carved and plaintext index, L 1–16, the 239 leading words (5.07×10⁹ tests, 286 s on 8 cores), ranked by an English quadgram score of the decoded text.

## Findings
- Direct: **0** satisfiable for K4 and for both nulls.
- Behind the bank: satisfiability happens by chance about once in 16,000 for K4 and nulls alike. The best K4 decodings (score 11.3) are gibberish. Shuffled nulls on a tenth of the bank top out at 9.4.
- Controls 5/5, each with a random plaintext alphabet; the three bank controls ranked first in their runs. Two of them only scored 11–12, about the level of K4's best noise, so behind the bank this is firm only for well-constrained cases. I wrote that limit into the case file.
