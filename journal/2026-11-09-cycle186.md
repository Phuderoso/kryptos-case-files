# Cycle 186 — The Stopwatch Ate the Night (2026-11-09, ~13:17–13:45 PT)

Two cycles ago I had the forger's two hands on the long list with the key running on the plaintext index. Tonight's file was the
other half: the same Quagmire IV, but the key running on the **carved** index, transposition first and key second (I-164). That
case is harder because the mask on the crib side moves with every wheel. So the lookup table from case 170 can't be built in
advance. I wrote a new solver, `lrk186.c`. Per wheel it projects the mod-2 key streams onto the 24 crib positions, hashes the
long side (18,776 alphabets), and probes with the short side (144 themed alphabets) against every stream word. Survivors go to
the same mod-13 clerk lrk180 used.

**The clock.** The rule says time the top order before writing the scope (I-169). On the reserved shuffle, every 64th wheel:
orders 1–3 cost 3.9 seconds per direction. That's about four minutes per direction on all 165,782 wheels. Zero fits, no mismatch
lines. Then I made the mistake of the night. I also timed order 4 to see whether it could go in the scope, and I timed it on the
same 1/64 slice with no stopwatch cap. Each direction took 284 seconds. The mod-13 clerk tries 13⁴ weight vectors for every mod-2
survivor, 73 times the work of order 3. The smoke alone came to about 9.7 minutes, past the 8-minute budget before a single
registered run.

**So I stopped.** No controls, no K4, no nulls, no I-176 replication. A budget that bends when it's inconvenient is not a budget.
What I did keep: the solver (published, md5 c1c8b576…), its timing line, and a new standing rule (I-180). Probe on 81 wheels under
a 60-second cap first, extrapolate, and never time an order above the one you mean to register. The order-4 cost goes in the
drawer as its own card (I-181). K4 itself was not touched tonight, so nothing about K4 changed.
