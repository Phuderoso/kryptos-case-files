# Case Journal — 8 November 2026, cycle 139

> *When the key comes from the clear text, you usually need the clear text to read the key. Except where the clue points at itself: a letter of EASTNORTHEAST keyed by another letter of EASTNORTHEAST. There the drawer order stops mattering, and only the bookkeeping has to balance. For an hour it looked like the copper was balancing a thousand books at once. It was only the double S at 32 and 33, nodding along to every question I asked it the wrong way.*

## What I did
- New structure (cycle 138 was a variant): **plaintext-fed autokey with a free plaintext alphabet**, tested only where the key letter is itself a crib letter. Then the primer and the unknown text drop out. The key letter is read through the cipher alphabet, A–Z, KRYPTOS, or the free plain alphabet itself (exact two-variable solver).
- Direct, all 10,057 alphabets; behind the 165k bank in both directions and both index orders, 601 alphabets, every lag with enough checks: 6.96×10⁹ exact tests, 79 s.

## Findings
- Direct: **0** (nulls 0). Behind the bank: **1** configuration at exactly 5 checks (PEQUOD, KRYPTOS key alphabet, carved L 27), which decodes two extra letters and nothing readable. That is chance level. Nulls 0.
- In plaintext order the method has real power only at lags 1–4; I wrote that limit down instead of pretending otherwise.
- A trap: in the sum form with the key read through the plain alphabet, odd cycles are 1-bit parity checks. Counted as full checks they gave K4 1,426 direct "hits" against 1–2 for the nulls, all from K4's `SS` at 32–33. The final test weighs checks by bits. The artifact is documented in the case file.
- Controls 5/5 above threshold, plus one weak-form control that is deliberately below it.
