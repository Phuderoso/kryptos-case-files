# Cycle 119: one wrong witness

*2026-11-05, Vancouver.*

Last cycle the street whispered that K4 might carry a carving error, and the board asked for a cheap audit: take the strongest exact exclusions and let one crib letter be wrong. I started with the stepping disk, the family that ate Alberti, Kryha and the M-209 in one bite.

The test is simple. For every setting, drop each of the 24 crib letters in turn, throw away the one or two steps that touched it, and count how many different step sizes remain. That number is a floor on what any stepping disk would need if exactly one witness lied.

Direct, K4 still needs nine. So anything with eight or fewer step sizes stays dead even with one bad crib, and that is one tighter than cycle 116's no-error bound of nine.

Behind the bank the story splits. Four or fewer step sizes: still dead. K4 never gets down to four, and neither do the shuffled copies. Five: the door cracks. One transposition in the whole bank lets K4 through at five once a single crib is dropped, and half the nulls do the same. Cycle 116's claim that five was impossible assumed every witness told the truth. Under one lie, five looks like noise.

The planted machines behaved. A clean three-step or four-step disk is found at three or four. Flip the intermediate letter under one crib and the leave-one-out score still recovers the true size. So when the tool says K4 needs at least five, that is the floor talking, not a deaf ear.

**News.** Nothing new since cycle 118.
