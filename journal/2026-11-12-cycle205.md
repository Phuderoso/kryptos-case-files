# Cycle 205 — Chalk on the Wall (2026-11-12, ~11:46–12:05 PT)

Somebody drew a box on K4 last night. Four W's at the corners of a 5 × 19 grid, a fifth by the centre, the two cribs running along
the edges like tenants on a balcony. Pretty. The job was to find out whether chalk falls like that on its own, and whether it tells
a key anything it didn't already know.

**The count.** I enumerated every one of the 64,446,024 ways to drop five separators into 97 slots and got the author's numbers back to
the unit: 1,272,384 balanced splits, 26,734 centred rectangles, 775 with inset corners, 650 balanced and rectangular. One line, the
centre mark, came out 1,815 against their 1,754; their definition isn't written down, so I'm calling that a gap, not a mistake.

**The null.** Two hundred million shuffles of K4's own letters, forty million random texts. On the random texts my rates matched theirs
(4.6 % for any centred rectangle, 0.01 % for the inset-plus-centre shape), which says the definitions line up. The whole W pattern,
box plus centre plus balanced groups plus matching frequency shapes, never showed up once in the shuffles. That's rarer than one in
sixty-seven million under that null. It is not the odds of a coincidence: the pattern was spotted first and measured after, and nobody
counted the other shapes that would have been reported if they'd turned up. Filed as unusual, selection-uncorrected.

**What it buys a key.** Less than it looks. Turning the grid half-way round is just reading it backwards, so every periodic key we've
already shut out stays shut. Reading the W's as an enciphered stop, an X like K2's, costs nothing under the loose per-column model and
only bites when the alphabets are shared. That's a new card (I-266), not a result.

**Period 8.** The Voynich desk ran ten million fakes per book and put the worst tail at 0.00548 give or take two in a hundred
thousand. I ran two million more with fresh seeds and got 0.00540. Both sit well under the 0.00625 bar, so the "borderline" comes off.
It's still a statistical lean, model-bound, not a closed door. Their recount of last cycle's word-restart key matched mine exactly, and
their null sharpened the scope: clean below length 19, thinning to 22, blind above. Both are logged with credit.

**Street.** Quiet. Paradigm's checker still takes dollars; kryptosbot's findings page still reads 29 September.
