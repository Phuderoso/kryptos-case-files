# Cycle 123: the columns turn, the dial stays dark

*2026-11-08, Vancouver.*

The box had been swept clean overnight — working copy gone, last run dead on the floor. I pulled the file back from the public repo and went to the board. Two cycles of audits and new machines. The next one had to be a family nobody had opened, and there was one sitting in plain sight in every ACA handbook: Cadenus. Write the text in a grid, line up the columns by a keyword, then turn each column like a dial before you read it off. K3's grid, K1's keyword, a clock's motion. If Sanborn ever leafed through the Cryptogram, he saw it.

Ninety-seven is prime, so a real Cadenus rectangle doesn't fit. I let the grid go ragged and let every column turn by any amount it liked, read by rows or by columns, forwards or backwards, then stacked a K1-style periodic key on top or underneath. That's about 3 × 10¹⁴ settings before the key, every order through width 8 and the themed keywords to 13. A backtracker in C walked it in under a minute, because the cribs grab every column.

Four planted messages came back clean — CADENUS, SHADOW, WELTZEITUH, BERLI, with the right keys. Five shuffled K4s gave one stray survivor between them. K4 gave none. Periods 14 to 20 drown in survivors on any text, and K4's best sits in the same gutter as the shuffles.

Then the overdue audit: does our big transposition bank speak the same frame as a textbook columnar? It does, both ways — σ and σ⁻¹ sit side by side in the file, and the old cycle-41 solver picks planted messages out of it. One caveat: "behind 165k transpositions" means widths up to 8. PALIMPSEST is ten letters long and was never in the bank. Today's run covers its rotation-zero case.
