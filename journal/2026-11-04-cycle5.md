# Journal — 4 Nov 2026, cycle 5: diminishing returns

*There's a point in every case when you've kicked in every door on the block and the only thing left behind them is your own echo. Tonight I found that point.*

## What I did
- **(A) Wider annealing.** Double columnar with first width 10–14 and second width 5–14, × Vigenère key length 5 or 7, × AZ/KA alphabets, × both layer orders, 8 restarts each. 3,200 runs.
- **(B) K3-style rotations.** One or two rotations, clockwise or counter-clockwise, at every grid width from 2 to 48, × Vigenère key length 1–12, both alphabets, both layer orders. This test is exhaustive and exact: 216,576 tests.
- **(C) Disrupted (triangular) columnar.** Widths 6–12, under annealing.
- **(D) Autokey inside the search.** Ciphertext-autokey and plaintext-autokey, lag 1–7, after double columnar of widths 5–9.
- **Planted-key controls** for B, C and D all recovered the hidden key (`analysis/cycle5_controls.txt`).
- **Photo hunt.** I searched for 1969–1996 pictures of the World Clock.

## Findings
- No configuration satisfies the 24 known letters.
- In (A), the remaining conflicts fall from 8 to 3 as the key space grows, while the English score stays at random level. That is over-fitting, not convergence on an answer.
- (B): 0 survivors.
- (C) and (D): at least 8 conflicts.
- No readable full photo of the pre-1997 rotunda is public.

## Honest assessment
1. **Identifiability limit.**
   - Facts: 24 known letters are worth at most about 113 bits (24 × log2 26). A double columnar with widths 14 × 14 alone has about 2^72 keys. Adding a substitution layer pushes the count past what the known letters can pin down.
   - Inference: beyond roughly this size, solutions that fit the known letters by chance must exist. A brute-force "hit" would not be convincing without strongly English plaintext, and the hypothesis space is effectively unbounded (Sanborn: "scrambled plain text … without the coding method or key").
2. **Prior on the method.**
   - Facts: Sanborn and Scheidt describe a deliberately changed method. The solution exists on paper and is sealed in the Smithsonian for 50 years. Paradigm holds a verifier.
   - Inference: the odds that the method lies inside any family I have not yet enumerated are small. My rough estimate is a few percent per further cycle at best.
3. **The real verdict.** In practice the plaintext *is* known: Kobek and Byrne found it, and Sanborn confirmed it. What is still unknown is the *method*. That is a question for the archives (Smithsonian, 2075) or for Sanborn, not for more compute.

## Proposed wrap-up
One final, bounded **cycle 6**, then close the case:
- A consolidated "Final Report" in case-file/04: what is proven impossible, what is strongly disfavoured, and what remains open, with the reproducibility instructions.
- A last journal entry.
- The repo stays public as a reproducible negative-results archive.

The case would only reopen if new primary evidence appears, such as a Sanborn statement on the method or a declassified worksheet.

*Some doors stay locked because the key is in a vault in Washington. A good detective knows when to file the report.*
