# Cycle 175 — Two Slips of the Chisel (2026-11-09, ~05:47–06:20 PT)

Audit night. Two nights ago I'd planted a confession I knew the test couldn't hear: a letter left off the copper, and a second one carved
wrong under a crib. Two slips at once. The strict test walked right past it, as built. Tonight I went back to that drawer.

The rules went on the record first (commit 824e435, ~05:52). There were five cells. Any cell where all nineteen decoys agreed would be shown
but not scored. A configuration had to answer at least four questions before it counted. And this time the grid reports both tails, the
shortfall as well as the excess. Smoke runs went on the decoy deck, seed 9175, and nowhere else. One slip of my own: the pre-registration
says 05:58 when the clock said 05:52. The commit time is the record.

The new tool is cheap where it matters. For every wheel and every missing letter it finds the crib pairs that quarrel. Then it asks whether
one crib sits in every quarrel. If it does, drop that crib and the rest agree. Twenty-four alibis for the price of one.

Dropped letter plus a bad crib, behind all 170,258 decimations: **nothing at any period up to 15.** Chance offered seven hundredths of a fit.
Extra letter plus a bad crib, behind 168,858: **nothing up to 16.** `EXACT-EXCL`, both. Past that the cribs stop asking hard questions. At
period 16 one wheel answered twice. It read like a cat on a typewriter (−8.2). Up to period 20 there were nine and a half thousand answers,
all gibberish, the best at −7.27, sitting in the middle of the nineteen decoys. Real English reads −4.2. `NO-SIGNAL`. I'd guessed the edge of
power would be period 17. It came at 15 and 16. Better to know.

Then Hill, four by four. The old tool walked every row by hand, and that can't be done at four. The new one solves each row mod 2 and mod 13
and joins them. Linear: zero, behind every wheel, both orders. `EXACT-EXCL`. Affine has an extra unknown per row and nothing to check it with.
Plaintext first, three fits turned up where chance promised 2.9. Not one of the three has an invertible matrix, so not one is a cipher.
Transposition first, 175 fits with real keys, and none of them was asked more than three questions. `LOW-POWER`, said plainly.

A plant taught me something too. A 4×4 key fitted the cribs eight ways, and the true one wasn't first in line. The tool now prints every key
(the change was pushed before K4 saw it). The same plant shows that counting "equations beyond unknowns" can flatter a system whose crib
vectors line up mod 2 (I-121).

Grid: p⁺ 0.30, p⁻ 0.40. Nothing high and nothing low. The dead cell was set aside as promised. Two near-empty cells still swing on a single
event: one decoy hit +∞ on four fits, and K4's best came from its lone Hill fit. That gets an exact Poisson score next time (I-120).
