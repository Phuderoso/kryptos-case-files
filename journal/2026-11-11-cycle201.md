# Cycle 201 — The Alibi Check (2026-11-11, ~19:42–19:56 PT)

Last night I put a frown on three doors: periods 8, 13 and 23, the cells the SAT runs left open. Tonight I went back to see whether the
frown would hold up in front of a judge.

**The witness who never changes her story.** Writing the tools, I noticed something I should have seen yesterday. With one alphabet per
phase, two letters of the same phase match in the ciphertext exactly when they match in the plaintext. The count I used is the
plaintext's count, whatever the alphabets. So the only real question is what plaintext K4 could hide. And K4's crib letters already say a
lot about it. Under period 8 they force five letters elsewhere, and they forbid 199 others: E, A, S, T at the very positions I was
counting. Yesterday's plants never heard that. That went into the registration (7107101, 19:49 PT) before the run.

**The first hearing collapsed on procedure.** I had borrowed the Voynich desk's "clean item scored as suspect" gate, but I set the bar
wrong: one bar per configuration, 48 configurations. Two came in at 0.085 against 0.081, and the pooled rate was a perfect 0.050. A bar
like that fails an honest test seven times in ten. By my own rule, run 1 is broken, with no verdict, and it stays in the file that way. I
fixed the bar (pooled, plus Bonferroni), registered that (672ce0d) and drew fresh seeds. There is no third run.

**The verdict.** Every gate passed, and yesterday's numbers came back to the digit. Carter, K1–K3 or Jane Eyre with X stops: the
plaintext model hardly matters. The conditioning does. Once the plants obey everything K4's cribs imply, K4's coincidences aren't
suspicious any more: worst q 0.090, 0.081, 0.096. **The three frowns are withdrawn.** The doors are open again, exact and statistical.
That's the job. A claim I can't defend comes down the same week it went up.

(q 0.001–0.023). At period 8, none of K4's 126 long-distance same-phase pairs match. I saw that after the run, so it's a lead, not a
