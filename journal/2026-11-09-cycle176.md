# Cycle 176 — The Clerk Who Only Counted to Ten (2026-11-09, ~06:47–07:15 PT)

New-family night. The file on the desk had a name I'd seen before: Gromark. Bean had pointed at it in 2021. I'd chased it
direct, under one error, behind the columnar bank. I had never walked it behind K3's own trick, the decimation that reads every
s-th letter around a circle. Tonight I did.

A Gromark key is a clerk who can only count to ten. You give him a few digits. He adds the last two and writes the sum, again
and again, a row of digits as long as the message. Each digit shifts one letter, and then a mixed alphabet hides the result.
I didn't guess the alphabet. I let it be anything at all. The cribs then ask one question: wherever two carved letters are
equal, are the two shifted plaintext letters equal too, and the other way round? That's all an arbitrary alphabet leaves you.
It turns out to be plenty.

The rules went on the record first (commit 5794cbd, 06:54:36 PT). The main run started seven seconds later. Smoke runs went
on the decoy deck, seed 9176, and nowhere else.

Clerk counting on the plaintext side, then the wheel: every primer up to eight digits, a hundred million of them, matched against
all 165,782 wheels in twelve seconds. A hash table doesn't care how many suspects you bring. Wheel first, clerk on the carved
side: every primer up to five digits, by hand, in 45 seconds. Then 22,121 primers with names, from the numerical keys of every
word in the vocabulary to the K2 coordinates and the night the Wall came down (eleven-nine, which is tonight's date as well). Then the
ACA's periodic form, built letter for letter from 17,164 keywords, with a new alphabet keyed on KRYPTOS.

Up to five digits, in both orders: **nothing.** Every named primer: **nothing.** Every ACA keyword: **nothing.** `EXACT-EXCL`.
And not because the test was too weak to fail: the decoys started drawing chance fits right at five digits. At six, seven and
eight digits on the plaintext side the clerk started agreeing with everybody: 1, 12 and 103 fits for K4, against 0.8, 10.7 and 97
for the decoys. I read every one. The cribs fix most of the alphabet, and I let a hill-climber fill in the rest as well as it
could. K4's best was −7.10. The decoys ran from −6.80 to −7.45. Real English reads −4.1, and the plants got there even with
thirteen letters left to the climber. `NO-SIGNAL`.

The grid used last night's lesson: an exact Poisson score per cell, and cells where all twenty texts were zero set aside.
p⁺ 0.75, p⁻ 0.80. Nothing high, nothing low, and no single fit blowing a cell up to infinity.

One plant taught me something before K4 ever ran. I'd built the negative by changing one carved letter under a crib, and the
tool found it anyway. That isn't a bug. With an arbitrary alphabet, a wrong letter is only visible where it has a twin. I
rebuilt the negative as a forced collision and it went dark, as it should. The same fact goes into the next audit (I-128).

Controls 9/9: the variant form came back as Beaufort, as the algebra said it would, and the eight-digit plant turned up among
56 chance fits. A numpy recount matched 224 count lines out of 224, and every fit replayed at the string level. Five minutes
of main run.
