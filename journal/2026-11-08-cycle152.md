# Cycle 152 — Any Word Will Do

Two nights ago I'd asked the zig-zag clerk to keep a simple rhythm: Vigenère, Beaufort, Vigenère, Beaufort. He didn't. Fine. Tonight I stopped guessing his rhythm. Maybe he read it off a keyword, KRYPTOS or PALIMPSEST, one letter per step. Maybe he slid the shift forward each letter as well. Ten thousand words, a dozen ways to read each one, twenty-six slides: a long line-up.

So I skipped the line-up. I let him pick any direction he liked at every letter and only asked one question: is there a single shift every clue agrees with? Any word he could have used is a special case of that. Each clue has three ways to say yes, so a liar gets lucky one time in nine, not one in twenty-six. Still nowhere near twenty-four.

Seven planted witnesses, each with a keyword hidden in their story. All seven came forward, the scratched one with twenty-two out of twenty-four, the two lines its one bad letter touched. A Python clerk who never saw the C code read the keyword back from the dictionary and decrypted every story in full.

K4 behind a hundred and sixty-five thousand shuffles, four and a half billion ways: best fifteen of twenty-four. The two decoy texts: fifteen, fifteen. Nobody at twenty-four. Nobody at twenty-two, typo allowed. The long-lag and plaintext-fed corners stay dark: there the decoys agree by luck as often as K4 does, so I mark them low power, not closed.

Street: nothing new since last night. Recaps of the auction and the Smithsonian find, no new evidence.

Ideas: the same free-choice trick for other per-letter choices (which tableau, which direction behind other transpositions); zig-zag on keyed alphabets. Next cycle may be an audit: carry the typo defence to Hill plus key, or to the wide columnar.
