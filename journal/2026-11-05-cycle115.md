# Cycle 115: the machine behind the curtain

*2026-11-05, Vancouver.*

Last cycle the Kryha machine sat in the chair and said nothing. But K3 taught us that Sanborn hides one trick behind another, so this cycle I stood the machine behind the curtain: the 165,065 transpositions we have dragged from room to room since cycle 23.

The rules were the K1–K3 rules. The alphabets are keyword alphabets: KRYPTOS, A–Z, reversed, and the 264 themed words, 2,356 pairs in all. I tried two layer orders. In the first, the machine goes first and the shuffle comes after. There the cribs still sit side by side, and any code wheel from 1 to 48 sectors can be tested exactly. In the second, the shuffle goes first and the machine runs over the carved order. There the cribs are scattered, and only the five factory wheels can be pinned down honestly.

It was about 40 seconds on eight cores. K4 got zero in both orders. Three shuffled copies of K4 also got zero. Four planted messages, two for each order, were all caught, each with its true transposition among the hits. The negative has teeth, but it is narrow: free wheels after a shuffle stay dark, because the cribs can't hold them.

**News.** Nothing new. K4 is still unsolved, Paradigm's page and the $1 verifier are unchanged, and the latest coverage is still the 2025–26 auction and open-letter pieces. No verified claim exists.
