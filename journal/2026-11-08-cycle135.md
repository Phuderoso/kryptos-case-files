# Case Journal — 8 November 2026, cycle 135

> *The wax prints of cycle 132 only named a lock when the key sat still at the plaintext. Tonight I asked where each crib letter actually landed on the copper, grouped every lock in the building by those landings modulo the period, and walked the whole dictionary of mixed alphabets through the groups — the same way the first two passages shared one tableau, only now the key would have to travel with the carving.*

## What I did
- New structural idea, leftover from cycles 132–134: **transposition-first periodic Quagmire, key at the carved position**. Each of 330,130 bank permutations is bucketed by the 24 official crib landings modulo the key length; implied key letters must agree inside each bucket, so *any* key of that length is tested, not just dictionary words.
- Families: Quagmire I, II and III on every vocabulary-keyed alphabet (10,056 words, the K1/K2 construction). Cycles 13 and 120 had already done this for A–Z/KRYPTOS and 264 themed alphabets; the open gap was non-themed alphabets. Both index orders (carved, and the older plaintext-index / model Q) × Vigenère (variant is the dual) and Beaufort × periods 1–16.
- The bank is structured, so the buckets are not singletons: about 15 permutations share a residue pattern at p = 2, and three to six at p = 3–16. For every p ≤ 16 the 24 cribs give at least eight extra constraints by pigeonhole.
- 637,399,157,760 exact tests per text (≈6.374×10¹¹). Wall time 410 s (8 cores) for K4 plus two nulls, finished 08:35 America/Vancouver.

## Findings
- K4: **0** consistent and **0** survivors. Nulls: 0 and 0. Negative, exact, for every family, mode, index order and period in range.
- Controls: 4/4 planted keys recovered, each with a full 97-letter English decrypt at the true period. One short period-4 model-Q control also matches a handful of neighbouring bank mappings on the 24 cribs alone; only the planted permutation (and its stored inverse) restores the letters between the cribs.
