# Cycle 203 — The Clerk Who Started Over (2026-11-12, ~03:45–04:20 PT)

Every period test in these files assumes the key marches on, letter after letter. A tired clerk might not. He could start his key
word again at the top of every word, the way you'd restart a hymn at each verse. Case 25 tried that trick at the X's back in cycle 26
and wrote "word boundaries: untested" in the margin. Tonight I went back for it.

**The rules first.** PREREG203 (local commit b972fc0, 03:47 PT) was written before K4 was touched: K1/K2's tableaux, any key, every
honest way to split EASTNORTHEAST and BERLINCLOCK into words, and the word holding each crib allowed to start earlier. The planted
clerks gave themselves up: ABSCISSA came back from a KA Vigenère, PALIMPSEST from a word-wise Beaufort, and SAT found a random
alphabet's plant.

**The verdict.** K4 has no word-restart key of any length 1 to 22 on the straight or KRYPTOS tableau. Not one configuration
survives, under any segmentation, and a thousand shuffled K4s never survive below length 20, so the test had teeth. The only
survivors from 23 up need BERLINCLOCK buried ten letters deep inside a single word. The word-wise key dies too, though the
shuffles die with it, so that says more about the cribs than about K4. With an unknown keyed alphabet (Quagmire III) the SAT
solver says no up to length 10 (Beaufort form 7); above that it's underdetermined, the way shuffles are. Case 190 has the
details. The heavy work came to about 9.5 minutes, a bit over budget, and the Quagmire null has only three draws. Both are noted.
