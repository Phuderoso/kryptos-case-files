# Cycle 227 — The other keyboard (2026-11-20, ~19:45–20:05 PT)

Case 210 asked K4 about the four keyboards everyone owns. Tonight was for the one hardly anyone owned. George Blickensderfer put the
ten commonest English letters on the home row in 1892, D-H-I-A-T-E-N-S-O-R, and sold it as "Scientific". The world bought QWERTY
instead. Sanborn likes old machines and old words. So: one more look.

**Slot.** New family (I-333 → I-327). PREREG227 went in at 19:49 PT before any run. Eight geometries the house had never tried:
DHIATENSOR read two ways (the keyboard wiki, and the No. 5 instruction manual's "G U T | B L E" split); a QWERTY board re-lettered
with KRYPTOS, PALIMPSEST and ABSCISSA, the K1–K3 way of keying an alphabet; and a finger that respects the stagger. Eighteen
keyboard alphabets checked against the 86,731 in the bank, all new. Also two house rules, sworn in before the counting: the I-330
bar (non-crib quadgram mean ≥ −6.0), and the Voynich desk's tie rule: when two crib seats split a key class, that class reads '?'.

**The work.** Seven minutes on eight cores. Gate T: the new tool counts byte for byte what the old one did. Plants (seed 209548):
twenty planted, twenty found, and all sixteen with a decrypt cleared the bar, wrong copper letters included. Then K4. Every
row / column cipher on the new boards: zero, nulls zero. Vector shifts and tableaux: zero up to periods 15–19 depending on scope, and
zero at every period read straight. Above that the shuffles hit as often as K4 does, so the method can't hear there. Three thin
K4-only cells one period below that line, all on the KRYPTOS board: four distinct crib tests, all gibberish (−7.9 to −8.3). 14.25
million K4 decrypts scored; none reached −6.0 (best −6.21). One shuffle did, −5.29, on exactly ten scorable quadgrams, the floor.
The bar's weak spot is the floor, not the threshold (I-335).
